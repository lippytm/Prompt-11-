# P-011-OMNI-001 — Omni-Device Multi-AI Broadband Learning, Publishing & Business Continuity Fabric

**Parent framework:** Prompt #11 — Unified AI Swarms Systems Factory  
**Related modules:** P-011-E6C-001, P-011-EEBDS-001, P-011-E6C-CHAR-001, P-011-DSF-001  
**Status:** Canonical architecture v0.4  
**Governing law:** Quality and Quality Assurance is Job #1.

> One project identity. One canonical checkpoint. Many devices, models, repositories, media forms, learning paths, and business experiments—with evidence, permissions, provenance, correction, and human approval preserved throughout.

## 1. Purpose

P-011-OMNI-001 converts Prompt #11 into a portable, resumable, cross-device and multi-AI operating system for the Encyclopedia of Everything Applied and the wider lippytm.AI Business of Businesses.

It is designed so authorized work can begin on an iPhone, continue on an iPad, move into implementation on an ASUS computer or lippytmai.zo.computer, be independently researched in Gemini/NotebookLM, reviewed or architected in Claude/Fabric Hermes, versioned in GitHub, coordinated through Slack, and resumed later without losing decisions, sources, permissions, quality status, or the next action.

This document defines architecture and operating rules. It does not claim that every connector, synchronization pathway, application programming interface, automation, or platform account is already configured or deployed.

## 2. Meaning of “broadband”

In this system, broadband means **high-variety, high-volume, multi-directional input and output**, not merely internet connection speed.

### Input families

- typed ideas, voice notes, dictation, and conversations;
- webpages, public records, research papers, source documents, and datasets;
- photographs, diagrams, screenshots, sketches, audio, and video references;
- code, repositories, issues, pull requests, tests, logs, and deployment records;
- Slack discussions, decisions, meeting notes, and collaboration requests;
- NotebookLM source collections and source-grounded research notes;
- business requirements, customer questions, campaign metrics, and financial assumptions;
- product defects, quality findings, incidents, corrections, and retirement decisions;
- fictional characters, environmental ecosystems, story worlds, and interactive branches;
- personal or restricted evidence only when placed in an approved private vault—not in public repositories, public AI chats, Slack channels, NFT metadata, or blockchains.

### Output families

- Encyclopedia entries and Character–Ecosystem Innovation Units;
- ebooks, audiobooks, video books, interactive videos, films, games, and simulations;
- programming lessons, code labs, repositories, tests, and deployment exercises;
- blockchain lessons, smart-contract labs, provenance systems, and governance exercises;
- AI agents, AgentBots, swarms, prompts, Brainkits, and bounded work packets;
- entrepreneurship curricula, customer-discovery exercises, offers, campaigns, and operating plans;
- workbooks, checklists, dashboards, evidence ledgers, quality packets, and certification records;
- accessibility editions, translations, instructor editions, affiliate editions, and archival editions;
- corrections, supersession notices, revocations, retirements, and succession packages.

## 4. Platform and device role matrix

A platform may perform several roles, but it must not silently become the only source of truth.

| Device or platform | Primary role | Secondary role | Prohibited assumption |
|---|---|---|---|
| iPhone | capture, voice dictation, triage, approvals, quick review | photographs, short edits, issue updates | that a phone-only conversation is the canonical project record |
| iPad | reading, visual review, annotation, storyboard work, presentation review | light editing, media review, quality signoff | that tablet edits are synchronized unless recorded in the checkpoint |
| ASUS laptop | coding, testing, repository work, file management, production review | local builds, deployment preparation, backups | that local files are safely backed up without verification |
| lippytmai.zo.computer | authorized runtime, cloud workspace, automation experiments, hosted tools | scheduled jobs, data processing, prototype deployment | that access or integration exists until credentials and tests confirm it |
| ChatGPT Business | orchestration, structured drafting, analysis, QA planning, continuation support | product architecture, source synthesis, task generation | that ChatGPT output is automatically verified or synchronized elsewhere |
| Gemini and NotebookLM | independent source-grounded research and notebook organization | audio overviews, question generation, source comparison | that repeated model output is independent evidence when it shares the same source |
| Claude and Fabric Hermes | independent architecture, long-context review, orchestration design, contradiction analysis | editorial review, work-packet routing | that Hermes has authority to approve truth, publication, contracts, or finances |
| GitHub | canonical version control, schemas, manifests, issues, pull requests, audit trail | documentation, test evidence, release records | that public GitHub is appropriate for private evidence, secrets, or personal records |
| Slack | team coordination, alerts, decisions, handoff notifications, discussion | links to authoritative artifacts | that Slack messages replace the canonical source, issue, decision record, or approval |
| NFT or blockchain systems | public provenance, edition identity, licenses, hashes, correction links | access credentials and collectible layers | that a hash proves the truth of the content or that a token guarantees value |

## 5. Canonical project hierarchy

The project hierarchy is:

1. **Prompt #11** — governing factory and quality system.
2. **Canonical modules** — E6C, EEBDS, OMNI, DSF, Character Continuum, and future approved modules.
3. **Programs** — publishing, learning, programming, blockchain, entrepreneurship, multimedia, safety, research, and franchise programs.
4. **Workstreams** — bounded collections of deliverables with owners and quality gates.
5. **Work packets** — device- and platform-portable units of authorized work.
6. **Artifacts** — documents, code, datasets, stories, media, tests, manifests, and reports.
7. **Evidence records** — sources, requirements, tests, defects, approvals, and corrections.
8. **Continuation packets** — the authoritative “resume from here” state.

## 6. Continuation Packet Standard

Every meaningful work session ends with one Continuation Packet. The packet is the portable checkpoint for moving between devices and platforms.

Required fields:

- packet ID and version;
- project, module, program, workstream, and work-packet IDs;
- session start and end timestamps;
- device and platform used;
- human owner and responsible reviewer;
- objective and scope;
- work completed;
- decisions made and decision rationale;
- artifacts created or changed;
- repository, branch, commit, pull-request, issue, and file references;
- source IDs and truth labels;
- privacy classification;
- model-line identity;
- quality level, RiskGate, and release-gate status;
- defects, blockers, unresolved questions, and dependencies;
- exact next actions in priority order;
- “resume prompt” written for a new session with no hidden context;
- correction, supersession, or retirement links when applicable.

The newest approved packet supersedes older packets but never deletes them.

## 7. Universal resume command

A user may resume by saying:

> **Continue Prompt #11 from the latest approved Continuation Packet. Preserve model-line independence, privacy classification, source provenance, Quality and Quality Assurance as Job #1, and begin with the highest-priority unblocked work packet.**

If the latest packet is unavailable, the system must reconstruct state from GitHub issues, pull requests, manifests, and the most recent archived packet. It must identify uncertainty rather than invent missing progress.

## 8. Broadband work-packet lifecycle

1. **Capture** — record the idea, request, source, problem, or evidence.
2. **Normalize** — convert it into a structured work packet.
3. **Classify** — assign privacy, truth, risk, rights, and model-line classifications.
4. **Route** — send only the minimum necessary data to authorized devices, platforms, people, or agents.
5. **Research independently** — ChatGPT, Gemini/NotebookLM, and Claude/Hermes produce separate first-pass work where required.
6. **Build** — create stories, curricula, code, media, business experiments, or prototypes.
7. **Test** — test facts, requirements, code, accessibility, security, rights, usability, environmental impact, and business assumptions.
8. **Compare** — identify agreements, contradictions, source dependence, and unresolved questions.
9. **QA** — assemble the Quality Evidence Packet and calculate certification eligibility.
10. **Human review** — approve, reject, restrict, or return for correction.
11. **Release** — publish only the approved edition and approved metadata.
12. **Measure** — collect learning, safety, environmental, audience, customer, and revenue results.
13. **Correct** — propagate corrections across every affected channel and edition.
14. **Evolve** — create an approved mutation, new edition, or improved process.
15. **Retire and archive** — withdraw unsafe or obsolete materials while preserving provenance.

## 9. Multi-AI independence protocol

Each primary model line receives:

- the same bounded objective;
- the same approved source manifest or a documented independent source collection;
- the same required output schema;
- the same privacy and rights restrictions;
- separate working folders, identifiers, and quality records.

The first-pass outputs must not be copied between model lines. Comparison begins only after each line completes its own work and QA review.

A model agreement score is not evidence quality. Evidence quality depends on source provenance, authentication, relevance, completeness, reproducibility, and contradiction handling.

## 12. Device continuity rules

- Begin each session by reading the latest approved Continuation Packet.
- End each session by updating that packet or creating its successor.
- Store important decisions in GitHub or another approved canonical system, not only in chat history.
- Use short capture notes on mobile devices, then normalize them into structured packets.
- Do not paste passwords, private keys, Social Security numbers, medical records, private evidence, or confidential witness data into general chat, Slack, public repositories, or NFT systems.
- Confirm synchronization before deleting any local file.
- Use conflict markers when two devices edit the same artifact; never silently overwrite an approved version.
- Record offline work as a queued packet and reconcile it before publication.
- Separate personal accounts, business accounts, model lines, and public-facing identities.

## 13. Security baseline

Every participating device and platform should use, where available and appropriate:

- strong unique credentials managed through an approved password manager;
- multifactor authentication, preferably phishing-resistant methods;
- device encryption, screen lock, current security updates, and recovery options;
- least-privilege access and separate roles for research, editing, approval, and deployment;
- repository secret scanning and no hard-coded credentials;
- encrypted backups and tested restore procedures;
- account, device, and clone revocation procedures;
- incident logging, credential rotation, correction, and notification plans;
- redaction before sharing across platforms;
- HumanApprovalGate for publication, minting, financial actions, legal or medical conclusions, and franchise certification.

## 14. Quality system

Every work packet records:

- acceptance criteria;
- evidence required;
- tests performed;
- reviewer identity;
- defects and severity;
- RiskGate status;
- applicable release gates;
- certification level;
- correction and retirement procedures.

### OMNI-specific release gates

- ContinuationGate
- SyncGate
- DeviceSecurityGate
- PlatformPermissionGate
- SourceProvenanceGate
- ModelIndependenceGate
- ConflictResolutionGate
- PrivacyRoutingGate
- AccessibilityGate
- RightsGate
- FunctionalGate
- LearningOutcomeGate
- BusinessIntegrityGate
- EnvironmentalGate
- HumanApprovalGate

A packet that fails ContinuationGate cannot be treated as the authoritative checkpoint. A platform that fails PlatformPermissionGate cannot receive the work packet. HumanApprovalGate cannot be passed by an AI agent.

## 15. Measurement

Measure more than volume and revenue:

- sessions successfully resumed without lost context;
- percentage of artifacts with valid provenance;
- unresolved conflicts between devices or model lines;
- learner completion and demonstrated skill;
- accessibility defects found and corrected;
- security incidents and time to containment;
- environmental impacts and mitigation;
- product defects, correction speed, and retirement quality;
- customer value, support burden, refund or complaint patterns;
- revenue, cost, margin, cash requirements, and reinvestment—without guaranteed outcome claims;
- mentor, contributor, and franchise-node quality.

## 18. Completion boundary

This architecture establishes the operating design. It does not itself configure device synchronization, create external accounts, connect private Gemini or Claude workspaces, deploy Zo services, create Slack channels, install software, transfer files, launch products, mint NFTs, or certify a franchise.
