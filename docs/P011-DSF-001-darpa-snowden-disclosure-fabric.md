## 2. Historical and institutional boundaries

### DARPA

DARPA is a United States defense research-and-development agency. Its mission centers on creating and preventing technological surprise. It develops and funds high-risk research; it is not interchangeable with the NSA, CIA, FBI, or a domestic police service.

Historical DARPA work includes technologies with both military and civilian effects. The Total Information Awareness / Terrorism Information Awareness initiative became a major privacy controversy. Congress imposed reporting and deployment restrictions in 2003 and later prohibited funding for the program as defined in defense appropriations, while preserving limited exceptions for lawful overseas military and foreign-intelligence activities.

### Snowden disclosures

Beginning in 2013, reporting based on documents supplied by Edward Snowden revealed or greatly expanded public understanding of NSA and allied surveillance activities. The disclosures led to declassification, litigation, legislative debate, oversight reports, operational changes, and continuing disagreement about national security, privacy, journalism, whistleblowing, and criminal liability.

Publicly documented subjects include:

- bulk telephone metadata collection conducted under the former Section 215 program
- Section 702 surveillance, including PRISM and upstream collection
- intelligence sharing among the United States and allied services
- government use of communications providers and network infrastructure
- minimization, targeting, querying, compliance, and oversight procedures
- the risks of incidental collection involving United States persons

The United States charged Snowden in 2013 under federal statutes concerning unauthorized disclosure of national-defense information, classified communications intelligence, and theft of government property. Those charges are allegations in an unresolved criminal case unless adjudicated. A separate civil judgment concerned his failure to complete required prepublication review for his memoir and speeches.

### Legal and policy consequences

The Privacy and Civil Liberties Oversight Board concluded that the former Section 215 bulk telephone-records program lacked a viable statutory foundation, raised constitutional and policy concerns, and showed limited value. Congress later enacted the USA FREEDOM Act, which ended that bulk program and established a more targeted framework.

The legal and operational status of Section 702 changes over time and must be rechecked before every publication. As of this module's review date, its statutory authorization had lapsed after temporary 2026 extensions, while existing court-approved certifications were reported to remain effective during a transition period. Any product discussing current law must carry a date stamp and link to the latest official or primary source.

## 3. Truth Boundary Protocol

Every factual unit must have one status label:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **VF — Verified Fact** | Supported by an authenticated primary record, final court finding, official report, or independently reproducible evidence. |
| **OA — Official Assessment** | A government or institutional conclusion that is authoritative but still open to review, error, or disagreement. |
| **CT — Corroborated Testimony** | Materially consistent testimony from independent sources, not yet fully verified. |
| **AL — Allegation** | A claim made by an identified source but not established as fact. |
| **WH — Working Hypothesis** | An analytical possibility requiring additional evidence. |
| **FD — Fictional Dramatization** | Invented or composite narrative material. |
| **CX — Contradicted** | A claim materially contradicted by stronger evidence. |

Required rules:

1. Never convert an allegation into fact through repetition.
2. Never treat an unresolved observation as proof of a preferred explanation.
3. Separate direct observation from interpretation.
4. Identify conflicts among official sources, witnesses, courts, advocates, and journalists.
5. Date-stamp all current-law, current-program, and current-conflict claims.
6. Preserve corrections and version history.
7. Use only lawfully available public, declassified, licensed, or original material.
8. Do not solicit, store, republish, or operationalize nonpublic classified information.

## 10. Release gates

A product cannot be published until all applicable gates pass:

- **SourceGate:** material claims have traceable sources.
- **TruthGate:** every claim has the correct evidence label.
- **DateGate:** current law, conflict, officeholder, and program status are freshly verified.
- **PrivacyGate:** no prohibited personal or medical data is exposed.
- **SecurityGate:** no secrets, credentials, exploit instructions, or sensitive operational details are present.
- **RightsGate:** copyright, publicity, licensing, and NFT terms are documented.
- **MedicalGate:** medical explanations receive qualified review and do not direct unsafe medication changes.
- **LegalGate:** identifiable allegations receive legal-risk review.
- **FictionGate:** composites and dramatizations are unmistakably identified.
- **AccessibilityGate:** ebook, audio, captions, transcripts, and interactive controls meet accessibility requirements.
- **RevenueGate:** pricing and affiliate claims are accurate and not exploitative.
- **HumanApprovalGate:** a responsible person authorizes release.
