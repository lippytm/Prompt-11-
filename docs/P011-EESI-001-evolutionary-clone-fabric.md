## 2. Meaning of “always getting smarter”

Continuous improvement is a controlled lifecycle:

`observe → evaluate → identify gap → propose mutation → test in sandbox → red-team → compare independent model lines → human review → approve or reject → version → monitor → correct, roll back, or retire`

“Always” means that the system retains a standing capability to learn from approved evidence and propose improvements. It does not mean:

- guaranteed unending intelligence growth;
- uncontrolled recursive self-modification;
- self-granted permissions;
- fabricated memories;
- autonomous deployment;
- autonomous financial authority;
- literal immortality;
- the right to override Charles Earl Lipshay or another authorized human.

## 8. Mutation lifecycle

1. **Gap detected** — evaluation shows a measurable difference between current and target performance.
2. **Evidence review** — confirm the gap is not caused by a defective benchmark or bad data.
3. **Mutation proposed** — define one bounded change and expected outcome.
4. **Risk classified** — Green, Yellow, Orange, or Red.
5. **Sandbox created** — use synthetic, public, or approved data.
6. **Tests written first** — acceptance criteria, regression tests, abuse tests, privacy tests, and cost limits.
7. **Independent alternatives** — model lines may propose different solutions without copying one another.
8. **Pilot executed** — no production authority.
9. **Results compared** — measure performance, side effects, cost, latency, and failures.
10. **Human decision** — approve bounded pilot, request changes, reject, quarantine, or retire.
11. **Version and lineage** — preserve parent, mutation, tests, decision, and rollback reference.
12. **Monitor and correct** — suspend or roll back on drift, incident, deception, or gate failure.
